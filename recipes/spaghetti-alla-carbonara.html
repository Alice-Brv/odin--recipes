SPAGHETTI ALLA CARBONARA

Italian Meatballs in Tomato Sauce (Polpette al Sugo) Tender Italian meatballs simmered slowly in a rich tomato sauce until juicy and infused with flavor — classic comfort food, perfect with crusty bread or pasta.

INGREDIENTS
  • 400 grams spaghetti
  • 150 grams guanciale, cut into small strips
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 1 large whole egg
  • 100 grams grated Pecorino Romano cheese
  • 1 teaspoons freshly cracked black pepper
  • 1 teaspoons fine salt, for pasta water

STEPS
  1. Boil the pasta: Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the 1 teaspoons fine salt, for pasta water — the water should taste pleasantly salty, since it also seasons the sauce. Add the 400 grams spaghetti and cook according to package instructions until al dente.
  2. Render the guanciale: While the pasta cooks, place the 150 grams guanciale, cut into small strips in a large, cold skillet and set over medium heat. Cook, stirring occasionally, until crispy and the fat has rendered out.
  3. Make the egg and cheese mixture: In a bowl, whisk together the 4 large egg yolks, 1 large whole egg, 100 grams grated Pecorino Romano cheese, and 1 teaspoons freshly cracked black pepper until smooth and well combined.
  4. Reserve pasta water and drain: Just before the pasta is ready, scoop out about a cup of pasta water and set aside. Drain the pasta, reserving the water.
  5. Combine pasta with guanciale: Remove the guanciale skillet from the heat. Add the drained spaghetti to the skillet along with a splash of pasta water, tossing to coat in the rendered fat.
  6. Toss in the egg mixture off heat: Off the heat, pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing continuously and quickly to create a creamy sauce. Add more pasta water a little at a time if needed to loosen the sauce.
  7. Serve immediately: Serve immediately in warmed bowls with extra grated Pecorino and cracked black pepper on top.

NOTES

The key to a silky, non-scrambled sauce is temperature control — remove the pan from the heat before adding the egg mixture, and use the residual heat of the pasta to cook it gently. Reserve extra pasta water, as different eggs and cheese absorb liquid differently. This dish waits for no one — serve it the moment it's tossed.
